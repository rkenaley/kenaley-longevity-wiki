1. Biophysical & Biochemical Mechanism

Long-lived extracellular structural proteins exhibit extreme half-lives: vascular and pulmonary elastin (t_{1/2} \approx 74\text{ years}) and interstitial/cartilage collagen (t_{1/2} \approx 15\text{--}117\text{ years}). Over decades, stochastic non-enzymatic Maillard reactions between reducing sugars/dicarbonyls (methylglyoxal [MGO], glyoxal, 3-deoxyglucosone [3-DG]) and lysine/arginine side chains form irreversible crosslinks—principally glucosepane (which accounts for >90\% of human dermal and vascular collagen crosslinks) and pentosidine.

A. The Mechanotransduction Senescence Loop (Piezo1 & YAP/TAZ)

  • Crosslinking increases the Young's elastic modulus (E, in kPa) of the basement membrane and interstitial ECM.
  • Resident stem cells, fibroblasts, and vascular smooth muscle cells sense elevated ECM stiffness via \beta_1-integrin focal adhesion kinase (FAK) and mechanosensitive Piezo1 cation channels.
  • Pathological matrix stiffness drives nuclear translocation of YAP/TAZ and MRTF-A, which epigenetically silence youth-associated chromatin domains, induce TGF-\beta$1 and LOXL2 (lysyl oxidase-like 2) fibrosis, and lock rejuvenated cells back into a senescent transcriptional state—even after parabiosis or senolytic clearance (14, 15).

B. Multi-Stage Biochemical Interception

To preserve low arterial Pulse Wave Velocity (PWV), glomerular basement membrane filtration (eGFR), and youthful stem cell mechanotransduction, we intercept glycation at three sequential kinetic steps:
1. Triosephosphate Shunting (Benfotiamine \to Transketolase): Intracellular Methylglyoxal (MGO) arises primarily from spontaneous \beta-elimination of phosphate from glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P) and dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP) during glycolysis. Benfotiamine increases intracellular thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) levels by $5\text{--}10\times, activating Transketolase (TKT) to shunt G3P and fructose-6-phosphate into the non-oxidative pentose phosphate pathway (forming ribose-5-phosphate and erythrose-4-phosphate), cutting intracellular MGO formation by >50\%.
2. Sacrificial Dicarbonyl Scavenging & GLO1 Activation (L-Carnosine + Pyridoxamine):
* Pyridoxamine traps Amadori intermediates and chelates redox-active transition metals that catalyze the conversion of Amadori adducts (fructoselysine) into advanced crosslinks.
* L-Carnosine (\beta-alanyl-L-histidine) acts as a sacrificial dipeptide nucleophile that reacts with MGO and malondialdehyde (MDA) faster than lysine residues on elastin/collagen, while Sulforaphane / SAC (11) induces Nrf2 \to Glyoxalase-1 (GLO1), which detoxifies MGO into D-lactate using catalytic Glutathione (16).
3. α-Dicarbonyl Crosslink Cleavage & LOXL2 Inhibition (Chebulic Acid + Rosmarinic Acid):
Chebulic acid (from standardized Terminalia chebula* extract) and Rosmarinic acid break \alpha-dicarbonyl-derived protein crosslinks in vitro and in vivo, inhibit RAGE (Receptor for Advanced Glycation End-products) NF-\kappaB signaling, and suppress LOXL2-mediated pathological collagen stiffening.

2. The Empirical Evidence Base

  • Hammes et al. (Nat Med 2003): Benfotiamine blocks three major pathways of hyperglycemic vascular damage and prevents advanced glycation end-product formation via transketolase activation.
  • Stitt et al. (Diabetes 2002): Pyridoxamine prevents retinal vascular acellular capillary formation and blocks collagen advanced glycation.
  • Al-Abed et al. (Bioorg Med Chem Lett 2004): Chebulic and rosmarinic acids break established \alpha-dicarbonyl protein crosslinks.
